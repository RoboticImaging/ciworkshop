01 Submissions

Call for Posters.

The first Australian Computational Imaging Workshop brings together researchers working across robotics, astronomy, medical imaging, signal processing, sensing and related disciplines.

We invite researchers and practitioners to share their work in the workshop poster sessions, fostering new connections and collaborations across the imaging community. Recently published and yet-to-be-published work is welcome. Poster presentations are non-archival.

Accepted extended abstracts will be made available to workshop attendees before the workshop begins, but will not form archival proceedings.

Topics of interest

We welcome contributions on computational imaging methods, technologies, hardware and applications, including but not limited to:

  • Inverse problems and image reconstruction
  • Diffractive optics and meta-optics
  • Neuromorphic and event-based sensing
  • Computational sensing and optical system design
  • Robotics
  • Astronomy and space technologies
  • Microscopy
  • Medical imaging
  • Signal processing
  • Flow visualisation
  • Photography and consumer devices
  • Remote sensing and geophysics
  • Material and industrial imaging
  • Computer graphics and interactive technologies

Submission

Submit a one-page extended abstract covering the research motivation, methods and findings, with relevant figures. References do not count towards the one-page limit.

Please include the names and affiliations of all authors in the extended abstract. Submissions will be reviewed single-blind: reviewers will see author information, but reviewers' identities will not be disclosed to authors.

An optional LaTeX template is available on Overleaf. Authors may use their preferred typesetting tool and layout. Submit the abstract as a PDF through OpenReview, and select the computational imaging domain most relevant to your work to help with reviewer matching.

Review

Submissions will undergo single-blind review through OpenReview. Each abstract will be read by more than one reviewer and assessed for relevance, quality and contribution to the workshop. Accepted authors will receive instructions for their poster and final extended abstract.

Poster format

Posters will be displayed digitally. A 16:9 landscape layout will fill the screen, but other poster formats are welcome. Please provide your poster as a PDF by email before the workshop, following the instructions in your acceptance notification.

There is no required poster template. You are encouraged to include the workshop emblem (SVG) on your poster.

Awards

A Best Student Poster Award (US$215) is planned. During submission, authors will be asked whether the lead author is a current student.

Travel support

Travel support for presenters may be available. During submission, authors will be asked: “Will you require travel funding support in order to attend the workshop?” Eligible applicants who answer yes may be contacted after acceptance with further details.

02 Community

Focused workshops & breakout sessions.

Propose a special-topic workshop, breakout session, or focused discussion group to share knowledge, upskill members, bridge disciplines, or focus attention on a particular problem.

During the second day of the workshop, a three-hour window will be allocated for these community-initiated sessions. The organising committee will select up to six topics from the proposals received.

The aim is to encourage collaboration across disciplines. We particularly welcome discussions on transferable methods, proposals organised by teams from different institutions, and sessions with hands-on participation. These are encouraged, but not required.

Let us know what you need to make your session successful, and we will do our best to accommodate.
