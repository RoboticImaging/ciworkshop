01 Submissions

Call for Posters.

The first Australian Computational Imaging Workshop brings together researchers working across robotics, astronomy, medical imaging, signal processing, sensing and related disciplines.

We invite researchers and practitioners to share their work in the workshop poster sessions, fostering new connections and collaborations across the imaging community. Recently published and yet-to-be-published work is welcome. Poster presentations are non-archival.

Accepted extended abstracts will be made available to workshop attendees before the workshop begins, but will not form archival proceedings.

Topics of interest

We welcome contributions on computational imaging methods, technologies, hardware and applications, including but not limited to:

  • Robotics
  • Astronomy and space technologies
  • Microscopy
  • Medical imaging
  • Signal processing and inverse problems
  • Neuromorphic vision and sensing
  • Meta-optics and diffractive optics
  • Flow visualisation
  • Photography and consumer devices
  • Remote sensing and geophysics
  • Material and industrial imaging
  • Computer graphics and interactive technologies

Submission

Authors wishing to showcase their work are invited to submit a one-page extended abstract covering the core motivation, methods and findings, with relevant figures. References are excluded from the one-page limit.

Please include all author names and affiliations in the extended abstract. Review will be single-blind, so authors should not anonymise the PDF.

Authors are invited to use the workshop's Overleaf template, but it is optional. Other layouts and typesetting tools are welcome. Extended abstracts must be submitted as PDF files through OpenReview.

During submission, you will be asked to select the domain of computational imaging most relevant to your work to support reviewer matching.

Review

Each extended abstract will undergo single-blind adjudication through OpenReview by the Program Committee and will be assessed for relevance, quality and impact on the workshop.

Authors of on-time submissions will be notified of the outcomes on or before Monday, 18 January 2027. Accepted authors will receive details about poster presentation and the camera-ready extended abstract.

Poster format

Posters will be displayed digitally on 16:9 screens in portrait orientation. Your poster presentation details will be provided in your acceptance notification.

There is no required poster template. You are encouraged to include the workshop emblem (SVG) on your poster.

Best Student Poster Award

During the poster sessions, members of the awards committee will judge student submissions for the Best Student Poster Award (A$300). When submitting your poster abstract, you will be asked whether the lead author is a current student to be considered for the award.

Travel support

Some travel support may be available to students and early-career researchers (ECRs) who are lead authors of accepted abstracts. During submission, you will be asked whether you would like to be considered for travel support and, if so:

  • What are you most looking forward to gaining from attending the workshop?
  • What additional funding is available from your university to support your attendance?
  • Would you be able to attend the workshop without travel support?

Travel support is limited and cannot be guaranteed for everyone. We will do our best to support broad attendance. If your institution or company can sponsor additional places, please contact the Sponsorship Chair.

02 Community

Focused workshops & breakout sessions.

Propose a special-topic workshop, breakout session, or focused discussion group to share knowledge, upskill members, bridge disciplines, or focus attention on a particular problem.

During the second day of the workshop, a three-hour window will be allocated for these community-initiated sessions. The organising committee will select up to six topics from the proposals received.

The aim is to encourage collaboration across disciplines. We particularly welcome discussions on transferable methods, proposals organised by teams from different institutions, and sessions with hands-on participation. These are encouraged, but not required.

Let us know what you need to make your session successful, and we will do our best to accommodate.
